Top 10 Things to Do in Pattaya with Kids (2026)

March 14, 2026 • 16 min read

Why Pattaya Is Perfect for Families

When most people think of Pattaya, family holiday might not be the first phrase that comes to mind. But that perception is well and truly outdated. In 2026, Pattaya has transformed into one of Thailand's most family-friendly destinations, boasting more kid-oriented attractions per square kilometer than virtually any other city in Southeast Asia.

What makes Pattaya particularly appealing for families is the concentration of activities. Unlike destinations where attractions are spread across vast distances requiring hours of driving, Pattaya keeps almost everything within a 30-minute radius. This means less time in transport and more time enjoying the experiences — a critical factor when traveling with young children who have limited patience for long car rides.

The city also delivers outstanding value for money. A family of four can enjoy a full day at a world-class water park, eat lunch, and take transport for less than what a single theme park ticket costs in most Western countries. Hotels in the family-friendly Jomtien and Na Jomtien areas offer clean, spacious rooms with kids' pools and play areas starting at THB 800-1,200 per night.

Add in the warm tropical climate (average temperatures of 28-34 degrees Celsius year-round), friendly locals who adore children, and the proximity to Bangkok (just 90 minutes by car), and you have a recipe for an unforgettable family holiday. Here are the 10 best things to do in Pattaya with kids, tested and approved by families who have experienced them firsthand.

1. Columbia Pictures Aquaverse

Ages: All ages (2+ for most attractions) | Duration: Full day (6-8 hours) | Cost: around THB 1,200 online (save over 25% off gate price of around THB 1,600)

Columbia Pictures Aquaverse (Google Maps) is far and away the number one kid-friendly attraction in Pattaya, and it is not particularly close. This movie-themed water park features 9 themed zones inspired by beloved Columbia Pictures films, with 20+ rides and water attractions that cater to every age group from toddlers to teenagers.

For the youngest visitors (ages 2-6), the Hotel Transylvania zone is a dream come true. Gentle splash pads, shallow wading pools, interactive water play structures, and regular character meet-and-greets with Mavis, Dracula, and friends create a magical experience. The area is entirely designed with small children in mind, with soft surfaces, shade structures, and easy access for parents to supervise.

Kids aged 7-12 will gravitate toward the Ghostbusters zone, which features medium-intensity water slides, interactive ghost-hunting water blasters, and themed play areas that bring the iconic film to life. The Jumanji zone adds an element of adventure with jungle-themed slides and water obstacles that feel like stepping into the movie.

Teenagers and thrill-seeking older kids will love The Beast, one of the most adrenaline-pumping water slides in Southeast Asia, along with the high-speed slides in the Bad Boys zone. Beyond water attractions, the park also offers go-karts, mini golf, and arcade games — perfect for when the kids need a break from the water.

The All-Inclusive ticket at ~THB 1,600 is highly recommended for families. It covers lunch and snacks throughout the day, which eliminates the stress and expense of buying food separately inside the park. The lazy river is perfect for the whole family to float together, and the wave pool generates gentle waves that even toddlers can enjoy in the shallow end.

The park opens daily from 10 AM to 6 PM and is closed on Wednesdays. Arrive early to beat the crowds and secure a good spot near the Hotel Transylvania zone. Free life jackets are available for children at multiple stations throughout the park. Book tickets online to skip the gate queue and save over 25%.

2. Ramayana Water Park

Ages: 4+ for most slides | Duration: Full day (5-7 hours) | Cost: ~THB 1,190

Ramayana Water Park is the second world-class water park in Pattaya, and having two such parks in one city is a genuine luxury for families. Spread across 184,000 square meters, Ramayana is one of the largest water parks in Asia by area and features over 50 slides and attractions.

While Aquaverse wins on theming and overall entertainment value, Ramayana excels in sheer slide variety and its beautiful Thai-inspired landscaping. The park is built into a hillside, which means many slides offer impressive drops and speeds that older kids and teenagers will love. The lazy river here is particularly long and scenic, winding through lush tropical gardens.

For younger children, Ramayana has dedicated kids' zones with age-appropriate splash areas and small slides. The aqua play fortress is a hit with kids aged 4-8, featuring tipping buckets, water cannons, and gentle mini slides. The park also has a large wave pool with a graduated entry that allows parents to wade in with toddlers at the shallow end.

Ramayana is located about 20 minutes from central Pattaya in the Sattahip direction. If your family has the time and budget for two water park days, visiting both Aquaverse and Ramayana on separate days gives you the complete Pattaya water park experience. For a detailed comparison, see our Aquaverse vs Ramayana guide.

3. Underwater World Pattaya

Ages: All ages | Duration: 2-3 hours | Cost: ~THB 500 (adults), ~THB 300 (children)

Underwater World Pattaya is a fantastic rainy-day option and a guaranteed hit with kids of all ages. The highlight is the 100-meter walk-through tunnel that takes you beneath a massive tank filled with sharks, rays, sea turtles, and hundreds of colorful tropical fish. Walking through the tunnel with marine life swimming overhead and on all sides creates a sense of wonder that captivates children and adults alike.

The aquarium features separate zones including a coral reef exhibit, a freshwater section showcasing Thai river species, and a touch pool where children can gently handle starfish, sea cucumbers, and hermit crabs. The touch pool is consistently one of the most popular spots with younger visitors, and staff members are on hand to guide the experience and answer questions.

Feeding times are scheduled throughout the day and are worth planning around. Watching a diver hand-feed sharks and giant groupers from inside the tunnel is both thrilling and educational. The facility is fully air-conditioned, making it a welcome refuge from the midday heat, and is compact enough to explore thoroughly without exhausting small legs.

Underwater World is located on Sukhumvit Road near the Jomtien Beach area, making it easy to combine with a morning or afternoon at the beach. It is an ideal half-day activity when you need a break from the sun or want something educational mixed into your holiday.

4. Nong Nooch Tropical Garden

Ages: All ages | Duration: 3-5 hours | Cost: ~THB 500 (adults), ~THB 300 (children)

Nong Nooch Tropical Garden is a 600-acre botanical wonderland that offers far more than just pretty plants. For kids, the highlights include the daily cultural shows featuring traditional Thai dancing and elephant demonstrations, the butterfly garden where hundreds of colorful butterflies land on outstretched hands, and the dinosaur valley section with life-sized dinosaur sculptures that thrill young paleontologists.

The garden is so vast that a tram service operates throughout, which kids enjoy as an attraction in itself. The cactus greenhouse is surprisingly engaging for children, featuring bizarre shapes and sizes they have never seen before. A replica of Stonehenge provides an unusual photo opportunity, and the 17-acre French Garden is genuinely stunning with its geometric patterns and vibrant flower displays.

For families with animal-loving children, Nong Nooch also has a small zoo section with birds, monkeys, and other animals. Pack a picnic or eat at one of the on-site restaurants. The gardens provide plenty of shade for hot days, and the paths are smooth enough for strollers (though a baby carrier may be easier for some of the hillier sections).

Nong Nooch is located about 15 minutes south of central Pattaya on the road toward Sattahip, making it easy to combine with a visit to Aquaverse, which is in the same general area.

5. Mini Siam

Ages: 3+ | Duration: 1.5-2 hours | Cost: ~THB 300 (adults), ~THB 150 (children)

Mini Siam is a miniature park featuring scaled-down replicas of famous landmarks from around the world, and it is far more entertaining for kids than you might expect. The park is divided into two sections: Mini Siam (Thai landmarks) and Mini Europe (international landmarks), together featuring over 100 miniature structures.

Children love spotting landmarks they recognize — the Eiffel Tower, the Statue of Liberty, Angkor Wat, the Great Wall of China, and Thai temples including Wat Arun and the Grand Palace. The miniatures are meticulously detailed, and kids enjoy the novelty of being "bigger" than famous buildings. It is essentially a geography lesson disguised as fun, and many children come away curious to learn more about the real places they have seen in miniature.

The park is relatively compact, which is perfect for younger children who tire easily. It is also one of the more affordable attractions in Pattaya. Visit in the late afternoon when the temperature cools, or in the early evening when the miniatures are lit up with colorful lights, creating a magical atmosphere that kids adore.

Mini Siam is located on Sukhumvit Road in North Pattaya, easily accessible by songthaew or Grab. It pairs well with a morning visit to Art in Paradise, which is just a short drive away.

6. Art in Paradise

Ages: 3+ | Duration: 1.5-2 hours | Cost: ~THB 400 (adults), ~THB 200 (children)

Art in Paradise is a 3D art museum where the artwork is specifically designed to be photographed with people as part of the scene. For kids, this is pure interactive magic. They can pose as if they are falling off a cliff, riding a dinosaur, floating in outer space, swimming with dolphins, or escaping from a shark — and the resulting photos look remarkably convincing.

The museum features over 150 paintings spread across themed zones including underwater, safari, fantasy, Egyptian, and classic art sections. Unlike traditional museums where children are told not to touch anything, Art in Paradise actively encourages touching, climbing on, and interacting with the art. This makes it one of the few museum experiences that kids genuinely enjoy from start to finish.

The entire facility is air-conditioned, making it an excellent option during the hottest part of the day or during rain. Most families spend about 90 minutes working through the exhibits and posing for photos. The best strategy is to have one parent take photos while kids and the other parent pose, then swap roles.

Art in Paradise is located on Second Road in central Pattaya. It is an ideal pairing with Mini Siam for a full day of indoor and outdoor exploration that keeps kids entertained without requiring swimsuits or sunscreen.

7. Teddy Bear Museum

Ages: 2-10 (best suited) | Duration: 1-1.5 hours | Cost: ~THB 300 (adults), ~THB 150 (children)

The Teddy Bear Museum is a uniquely Pattaya attraction that younger children, in particular, find absolutely enchanting. The museum features thousands of teddy bears arranged in themed rooms including an ice cave, an Inca temple, a European village, a space station, a Thai village, and more.

Each room tells a story through its teddy bear displays, and kids love searching for their favorite bears and posing for photos alongside the furry characters. The attention to detail in each themed room is impressive, with elaborate sets, lighting effects, and even sound effects creating immersive environments that feel like stepping into different worlds.

While teenagers might find the concept a bit young for their tastes, children aged 2-10 consistently rate this as one of their favorite Pattaya experiences. The museum is fully air-conditioned and compact, requiring minimal walking — perfect for toddlers who cannot manage a full day of trekking between attractions.

The Teddy Bear Museum is located near the intersection of Sukhumvit Road and Central Pattaya Road. It makes an excellent morning or afternoon activity and pairs well with nearby restaurants for a family lunch.

8. Khao Kheow Open Zoo

Ages: All ages | Duration: 4-6 hours (full day with night safari) | Cost: ~THB 300 (adults), ~THB 150 (children); Night Safari additional

Khao Kheow Open Zoo is one of the finest zoos in Southeast Asia and a must-visit for animal-loving families. Located about 30 minutes from central Pattaya, this sprawling facility covers over 2,000 acres of natural habitat and houses more than 300 species of animals. Unlike traditional zoos with small enclosures, Khao Kheow gives animals expansive, naturalistic habitats that allow them to behave more naturally.

The zoo is organized into zones that you can explore by driving your own car, taking a tram, or renting a golf cart (highly recommended for families as it gives you freedom to stop wherever you like). Highlights include the giraffe feeding station where kids can hand-feed giraffes at eye level, the penguin house (a welcome blast of air conditioning), the tiger enclosure with viewing platforms, and the flamingo lake.

The Night Safari is a particular highlight and worth the extra cost. A guided tram ride takes you through the zoo after dark, when nocturnal animals are most active. Seeing tigers, hippos, deer, and other animals under moonlight is a magical experience that most children remember for years. The night safari typically starts at around 6 PM and includes a light show.

Pack snacks and drinks for the visit, as the zoo is large and exploring works up an appetite. Comfortable shoes are essential if you plan to walk any sections. The zoo is easily reached by Grab or private car from Pattaya, and many hotels can arrange transport.

9. Jomtien Beach

Ages: All ages | Duration: Half day to full day | Cost: Free (sunbed rental ~THB 100-200)

No family holiday in Pattaya is complete without spending time at Jomtien Beach, the most family-friendly beach in the area. Stretching for 6 kilometers south of Pattaya Beach, Jomtien offers a noticeably more relaxed atmosphere with calmer waters, gentler slopes into the sea, and fewer jet skis than the main Pattaya Beach strip.

The beach is wide enough that even during busy periods, you can find a spot with plenty of space for kids to build sandcastles and play. The water along Jomtien is generally calm and shallow near the shore, making it suitable for younger children to paddle. However, adult supervision is always essential, and strong swimmers should still exercise caution further from shore.

Beachfront restaurants along Jomtien Beach Road serve fresh seafood, Thai dishes, and child-friendly options like fried rice, chicken satay, and fresh fruit smoothies. Many restaurants have outdoor seating where you can watch the kids play on the sand while enjoying a meal. Sunbed and umbrella rentals are available for THB 100-200 per set.

For families wanting water activities, Jomtien offers banana boat rides, sea kayaks, and stand-up paddleboards at reasonable prices. The southern end of Jomtien near the Aquaverse area is particularly peaceful. Consider combining a morning at the beach with an afternoon at Underwater World, which is located nearby on Sukhumvit Road.

10. Cartoon Network Amazone (Now Aquaverse)

Note: This park has been rebranded as Columbia Pictures Aquaverse (see #1 above)

If you have been researching Pattaya water parks, you may come across references to Cartoon Network Amazone. It is important to know that this park no longer operates under the Cartoon Network name. The facility has been completely reimagined and relaunched as Columbia Pictures Aquaverse, which we covered in detail as our number one recommendation above.

The transformation from Cartoon Network Amazone to Aquaverse brought significant upgrades. The movie-themed zones replaced the cartoon themes, new attractions were added including go-karts and mini golf, character experiences expanded to include favorites from Ghostbusters, Hotel Transylvania, Jumanji, and other Columbia Pictures films, and the overall entertainment value increased substantially.

If you previously visited Cartoon Network Amazone and wondered about returning, the answer is a definitive yes. The park is a completely different experience now, with more zones, more rides, better facilities, and the same convenient location in the Na Jomtien area. Online tickets start at ~THB 1,200 (over 25% off the gate price of around THB 1,600), and the All-Inclusive option at ~THB 1,600 is particularly good value for families.

The location remains the same, at KM 5 on Sukhumvit Road in the Na Jomtien area, making it easy to reach from any Pattaya hotel. Open daily from 10 AM to 6 PM, closed Wednesdays.

3-Day Family Itinerary

This itinerary is designed to give your family the best of Pattaya with kids while maintaining a relaxed pace that does not exhaust little legs. Adjust as needed based on your children's ages and energy levels.

Day 1: Aquaverse Water Park

  • 9:30 AM: Arrive at Aquaverse before gates open at 10 AM to secure a good base near the Hotel Transylvania zone
  • 10:00 AM - 12:30 PM: Explore kid-friendly zones — Hotel Transylvania for younger kids, Ghostbusters and Jumanji for older kids
  • 12:30 PM - 1:30 PM: Lunch break (included with All-Inclusive ticket at ~THB 1,600)
  • 1:30 PM - 4:00 PM: Lazy river, wave pool, and remaining slides
  • 4:00 PM - 5:30 PM: Go-karts, mini golf, and wind-down activities
  • 6:00 PM: Dinner at a beachfront restaurant in Na Jomtien

Day 2: Culture and Nature

  • 9:00 AM - 12:00 PM: Nong Nooch Tropical Garden — gardens, cultural show, and butterfly house
  • 12:00 PM - 1:00 PM: Lunch at Nong Nooch or nearby restaurant
  • 1:30 PM - 3:30 PM: Art in Paradise 3D museum
  • 4:00 PM - 5:30 PM: Mini Siam (arrive for the late-afternoon light show)
  • 6:00 PM: Dinner at Thepprasit Night Market (if visiting on Friday, Saturday, or Sunday)

Day 3: Beach and Marine Life

  • 9:00 AM - 12:00 PM: Jomtien Beach — swimming, sandcastles, and water activities
  • 12:00 PM - 1:00 PM: Lunch at a Jomtien Beach Road restaurant
  • 1:30 PM - 3:30 PM: Underwater World Pattaya — tunnel walk, touch pool, and feeding times
  • 4:00 PM - 6:00 PM: Free time for hotel pool, shopping, or Teddy Bear Museum
  • 6:30 PM: Farewell dinner at a family-friendly Thai restaurant

Optional Day 4: If you have a fourth day, spend it at Khao Kheow Open Zoo, including the Night Safari in the evening. Alternatively, take the ferry to Koh Larn island for a beach day with crystal-clear water, or visit Ramayana Water Park for a second day of water fun. Family Travel Tips for Pattaya

  1. Book Aquaverse online: Save over 25% by purchasing tickets at around THB 1,200 online versus around THB 1,600 at the gate. The All-Inclusive ticket (~THB 1,600) pays for itself by including lunch and snacks.
  2. Stay in Jomtien or Na Jomtien: These areas are the most family-friendly, with calm beaches, kid-friendly restaurants, and proximity to Aquaverse. Central Pattaya has more nightlife and less suitable vibes for families. See our hotel guide.
  3. Pack sun protection: The Thai sun is intense. Bring SPF 50+ sunscreen (reef-safe preferred), UV rash guards for the kids, hats, and sunglasses. Reapply sunscreen every 2 hours, especially at water parks.
  4. Carry rehydration sachets: Heat and humidity can cause dehydration quickly in children. Carry oral rehydration salts and ensure kids drink water regularly throughout the day, not just when they feel thirsty.
  5. Use Grab for transport: Grab (the Southeast Asian equivalent of Uber) is the easiest way to get around Pattaya with kids. It eliminates negotiating with taxi drivers and lets you install car seats in advance for younger children.
  6. Eat where locals eat: Night markets and local restaurants serve the freshest food at the best prices. Most Thai street food is cooked fresh to order, making it safer than buffet-style options. Fried rice, chicken satay, and grilled meats are reliably kid-friendly choices.
  7. Schedule rest time: Do not try to cram too many activities into one day. Plan for a midday break at the hotel, especially during hot season (March-May). Overstimulated, exhausted kids make for miserable family holidays.
  8. Know your medical options: Bangkok Hospital Pattaya provides excellent international-standard healthcare with English-speaking staff. Save their number in your phone: +66 (0) 38 259 999. Pharmacies are plentiful and well-stocked throughout the city.
  9. Avoid Wednesdays for Aquaverse: The park is closed every Wednesday. Plan your water park day for another day of the week.
  10. Bring waterproof phone cases: Essential for water parks and beach days. Being able to take photos and videos of the kids on slides and in the water without worrying about your phone is priceless.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Pattaya good for kids?

Yes, Pattaya is excellent for kids. The city offers world-class water parks like Aquaverse (around THB 1,200 online), interactive museums, an aquarium, botanical gardens, a zoo, and family-friendly beaches. The Jomtien and Na Jomtien areas are particularly family-oriented with calm waters and kid-friendly restaurants.

What is the best age for Aquaverse?

Aquaverse caters to all ages. Children under 3 enter free. The Hotel Transylvania zone is perfect for toddlers and young kids (ages 2-6) with gentle splash areas and character meet-and-greets. Older kids (7-12) love the medium-intensity slides and Ghostbusters zone. Teenagers enjoy The Beast and other thrill rides. The All-Inclusive ticket (~THB 1,600) is ideal for families as it covers lunch and snacks.

How many days in Pattaya with kids?

We recommend 3-4 days in Pattaya with kids. Day 1 for Aquaverse water park, Day 2 for cultural attractions like Nong Nooch Garden and Mini Siam, and Day 3 for Jomtien Beach and Underwater World. An optional Day 4 can include Khao Kheow Open Zoo or Ramayana Water Park.

Is Pattaya safe for families?

Yes, Pattaya is safe for families. The Jomtien, Na Jomtien, and Wongamat areas are family-friendly neighborhoods well removed from the nightlife zones. Tourist police are active and helpful. Bangkok Hospital Pattaya provides international-standard healthcare. Standard travel precautions apply as with any tourist destination.

What should kids wear to Aquaverse?

Children should wear swimsuits or rash guards with UV protection. Waterproof sandals or aqua shoes are recommended for walking around the park. Bring a change of dry clothes, sunscreen (SPF 50+), hats, and reusable water bottles. Life jackets are provided free at the park for younger children.

Are there kid-friendly hotels near Aquaverse?

Yes, the Na Jomtien and Jomtien areas near Aquaverse have many family-friendly hotels with kids' pools, play areas, and connecting rooms. Some hotels arrange transfers to Aquaverse for an extra charge. Budget family options start at THB 800-1,200 per night, while mid-range family resorts run THB 1,500-3,000 per night.
